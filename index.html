What it does

New in 0.3.0: Fix for 1.20

Bring an older mod to 1.20, on its own

Pick a mod made for an older version of the game and choose Fix for 1.20…. CrusaderPope reads the mod against the game, works out what 1.20 changed underneath it and writes a patch mod that loads right after it; the mod itself stays as it is. It won’t settle every edge case, and it tells you which ones are left; for nearly everything else it gets you most of the way there.

  • What 1.20 renamed or moved is converted, also where the mod’s scripts, events, interface files and texts use it
  • The mod’s old copies of game files are brought up to date without touching its own values
  • The mod’s graphics are fitted into the game’s new shaders
  • A plan first; run it again after a game or mod update

Readable view

Script, turned into plain language

Every entry gets a Summary that reads like the game itself: “Gains 50 Prestige”, “If the vassal has the trait Brave: …”. Events become a timeline of their text, conditions and options; traits, decisions, interactions and titles show what they do, not how they are written.

  • Events, decisions, traits, interactions, laws, faiths, rites, cultures, characters, titles, on_actions, scripted effects …
  • The game’s own effect texts with their values; conditions that say whom they are about
  • Game icons and event illustrations right where they belong

Cross-references

Who uses what — one hover away

Every name is a link. Hover it for a preview card; middle-click pins the card so you can follow its links in turn. The References tab shows everything an entry uses and everything that uses it.

  • Back and forward (mouse buttons, Alt+arrows) return to the page, tab and scroll position you left
  • Who is who in an event and what fires it: the on_actions, events and decisions that lead to it
  • One search across 500,000+ entries and localization keys (Ctrl+K)

Expert views

The script, when you want it

Under Expert: the Source exactly as the game has it, with its file and lines, and a Graph of what leads into an entry and where it leads.

  • Open the file in your editor or reveal it in Explorer
  • Graph depth, flow-only and localization filters

Edit in place

The readable view is the editor

Pick a mod to edit, then change entries right where you read them: click a line to change it, add a new one or remove it. A keyboard-driven picker knows the game’s statements, scopes and values, explains every pick in plain language, and writes the Paradox script for you.

  • “Override in <mod>” copies a game entry into your mod; new entries and duplicates work the same way
  • The picker offers saved scopes and lists only where the script has them
  • Texts edited where they show, with text codes and examples of what they return
  • Every change undoable (and redoable); comments and layout of your script kept

3D portraits & barbershop

Historical characters, with their faces

Characters are built in 3D from the game’s own DNA, genes, clothes and shaders, next to their stats, traits, family and life events, at any date you pick. The Barbershop changes a character’s looks gene by gene and saves the DNA into your mod.

  • The game’s own shaders (or a mod’s), recompiled for WebGL
  • Add life events, parents, siblings, twins and children
  • Galleries for every image and 3D model, coats of arms drawn as in the game

Map

The world at any bookmark date

A 2D and 3D map with realms, de jure hegemonies, cultures, faiths and holdings at any date you pick, drawn with the game’s terrain, trees, water and buildings, or as a paper map.

  • Slide through history; every realm, culture and faith listed with its share
  • Castles, cities and temples as they stand at the date; fog of war as the game draws it
  • Create and edit bookmarks

Mods

Your playsets, and where mods collide

CrusaderPope reads the launcher’s mods and playsets and shows exactly what each loaded mod adds, changes or removes, and whether a mod’s copy is the same as the game’s, a duplicate the game won’t pick, or a real conflict.

  • Create, merge and delete mods, manage playsets, pack and unpack
  • Playsets written back to the launcher, with backups
  • Switching back to a mod list you used before takes seconds

Experimental

Interface files and game saves

A GUI tab browses the game’s windows, shows where they are used and previews them as the game draws them; widgets can be edited into your mod. The Save page opens a game save and changes its characters into a new save file.

  • Both are experimental: check their results in the game

Get it

1

Download

From the releases page: the setup (.exe, no administrator rights needed) or the portable .zip; for Linux an AppImage or a .deb.

2

Start

A short setup finds your Crusader Kings III installation and your mods folder by itself, and lets you pick the graphics quality.

3

Explore & edit

The first start reads the whole game, later ones load from a cache. Pick a mod to edit and change entries right where you read them.

Requirements

  • Windows 10/11; Linux builds are new and little tested, macOS is untested.
  • A legal copy of Crusader Kings III — the tool reads the base game files and is useless without them. Made for 1.20.1; the coming 1.21 isn't supported yet.
  • A reasonably strong PC: the full index takes around 2 GB of memory, more with big mods.

Running from source? See INSTALL.md.

Free, forever

CrusaderPope is free and open source, and it will stay that way. Its license (GPL-3.0 with the Commons Clause) forbids selling it — and anyone putting a fork behind a “donation” paywall may always get wet shirt sleeves after washing their hands. Even with short sleeves.

It was built with AI (Anthropic's Claude), openly and plainly — and CrusaderPope itself will never include any AI functions. The whole story is in the README.
